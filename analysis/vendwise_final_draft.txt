Introduction
 
 
Small
 
and
 
medium-sized
 
enterprises
 
(SMEs)
 
form
 
the
 
backbone
 
of
 
developing
 
economies,
 
contributing
 
significantly
 
to
 
employment
 
and
 
GDP
 
growth.
 
In
 
the
 
Philippines,
 
micro,
 
small,
 
and
 
medium
 
enterprises
 
account
 
for
 
99.5%
 
of
 
all
 
businesses
 
and
 
employ
 
approximately
 
63%
 
of
 
the
 
workforce
 
(Department
 
of
 
Trade
 
and
 
Industry,
 
2021).
 
Despite
 
their
 
economic
 
importance,
 
many
 
small
 
retail
 
businesses,
 
particularly
 
street
 
vendors,
 
sari-sari
 
stores,
 
and
 
market
 
stalls,
 
continue
 
to
 
operate
 
using
 
traditional
 
pen-and-paper
 
methods
 
for
 
sales
 
transactions
 
and
 
inventory
 
management.
 
This
 
reliance
 
on
 
manual
 
processes
 
creates
 
numerous
 
operational
 
challenges
 
that
 
hinder
 
business
 
efficiency
 
and
 
growth
 
potential.
 
The
 
digitalization
 
gap
 
in
 
small
 
retail
 
operations
 
stems
 
from
 
several
 
interconnected
 
factors.
 
Research
 
by
 
Rahayu
 
and
 
Day
 
(2015)
 
indicates
 
that
 
cost
 
barriers,
 
technological
 
complexity,
 
and
 
limited
 
digital
 
literacy
 
prevent
 
many
 
small
 
business
 
owners
 
from
 
adopting
 
point-of-sale
 
(POS)
 
systems.
 
Traditional
 
POS
 
solutions
 
typically
 
require
 
expensive
 
hardware
 
such
 
as
 
cash
 
registers,
 
barcode
 
scanners,
 
and
 
receipt
 
printers,
 
making
 
them
 
financially
 
inaccessible
 
for
 
micro-enterprises
 
operating
 
on
 
thin
 
profit
 
margins.
 
Furthermore,
 
many
 
existing
 
POS
 
applications
 
are
 
designed
 
with
 
large
 
retail
 
chains
 
in
 
mind,
 
featuring
 
complex
 
interfaces
 
and
 
functionalities
 
that
 
overwhelm
 
small
 
business
 
owners
 
who
 
need
 
straightforward,
 
practical
 
tools
 
(Kurnia
 
et
 
al.,
 
2015).
 
Manual
 
bookkeeping
 
introduces
 
significant
 
operational
 
inefficiencies
 
and
 
errors.
 
Studies
 
demonstrate
 
that
 
human
 
calculation
 
errors
 
occur
 
in
 
approximately
 
3-5%
 
of
 
manual
 
transactions
 
(Reason,
 
1990),
 
which,
 
when
 
accumulated
 
over
 
hundreds
 
of
 
daily
 
transactions,
 
result
 
in
 
substantial
 
revenue
 
loss
 
and
 
inventory
 
discrepancies.
 
Additionally,
 
handwritten
 
records
 
are
 
vulnerable
 
to
 
physical
 
damage,
 
loss,
 
and
 
illegibility,
 
making
 
historical
 
sales
 
analysis
 
nearly
 
impossible.
 
Small
 
business
 
owners
 
consequently
 
lack
 
visibility
 
into
 
critical
 
business
 
metrics
 
such
 
as
 
best-selling
 
products,
 
slow-moving
 
inventory,
 
peak
 
sales
 
periods,
 
and
 
profit
 
margins—information
 
that
 
is
 
essential
 
for
 
informed
 
decision-making
 
and
 
business
 
growth
 
(Laudon
 
&
 
Laudon,
 
2016).
 
The
 
proliferation
 
of
 
smartphones
 
presents
 
an
 
unprecedented
 
opportunity
 
to
 
bridge
 
this
 
digitalization
 
gap.
 
Mobile
 
phone
 
penetration
 
in
 
developing
 
countries
 
has
 
reached
 
83%,
 
with
 
smartphone
 
adoption
 
growing
 
rapidly
 
even
 
among
 
low-income
 
populations
 
(GSMA,
 
2022).
 
This
 
widespread
 
mobile
 
device
 
ownership
 
creates
 
a
 
platform
 
for
 
delivering
 
affordable,
 
accessible
 
business
 
management
 
tools
 
without
 
requiring
 
additional
 
hardware
 
investments.
 
Mobile-first
 
applications
 
that
 
function
 
offline
 
address
 
connectivity
 
challenges
 
prevalent
 
in
 
developing
 
regions
 
while
 
providing
 
the
 
computational
 
power
 
needed
 
for
 
transaction
 
processing
 
and
 
data
 
management
 
(Qiang
 
et
 
al.,
 
2012).
 
Research
 
on
 
technology
 
acceptance
 
among
 
small
 
businesses
 
emphasizes
 
the
 
importance
 
of
 
simplicity,
 
perceived
 
usefulness,
 
and
 
minimal
 
disruption
 
to
 
existing
 
workflows
 
(Davis,
 
1989;
 
Venkatesh
 
&
 
Davis,
 
2000).
 
Small
 
business
 
owners
 
are
 
more
 
likely
 
to
 
adopt
 
technological
 
solutions
 
that
 
demonstrate
 
immediate,
 
tangible
 
benefits—such
 
as
 
faster
 
checkout
 
times,
 
reduced
 
errors,
 
and
 
clearer
 
sales
 
insights—without
 
requiring
 
extensive
 
training
 
or
 
significant
 
changes
 
to
 
their
 
operational
 
processes.
 
Mobile
 
applications
 
that
 
prioritize
 
intuitive
 
design,
 
minimal
 
data
 
entry,
 
and
 
automatic
 
calculation
 
can
 
overcome
 
adoption
 
barriers
 
by
 
fitting
 
seamlessly
 
into
 
existing
 
business
 
routines.

Despite
 
the
 
clear
 
need
 
for
 
accessible
 
digital
 
business
 
management
 
tools,
 
a
 
significant
 
gap
 
exists
 
in
 
the
 
market
 
for
 
lightweight,
 
offline-capable
 
POS
 
and
 
inventory
 
management
 
solutions
 
specifically
 
designed
 
for
 
small-scale
 
retailers
 
in
 
developing
 
economies.
 
Most
 
available
 
applications
 
either
 
require
 
constant
 
internet
 
connectivity,
 
demand
 
powerful
 
smartphone
 
hardware,
 
or
 
charge
 
recurring
 
subscription
 
fees
 
that
 
are
 
prohibitive
 
for
 
micro-enterprises.
 
There
 
is
 
a
 
pressing
 
need
 
for
 
a
 
mobile
 
application
 
that
 
combines
 
transaction
 
recording,
 
inventory
 
tracking,
 
and
 
sales
 
reporting
 
in
 
a
 
simple,
 
affordable
 
package
 
that
 
works
 
reliably
 
on
 
entry-level
 
and
 
mid-range
 
smartphones
 
with
 
intermittent
 
internet
 
access.
 
VendWise
 
is
 
developed
 
to
 
address
 
these
 
specific
 
challenges
 
facing
 
small
 
retail
 
businesses.
 
The
 
application
 
provides
 
an
 
integrated
 
solution
 
for
 
point-of-sale
 
operations,
 
inventory
 
management,
 
and
 
sales
 
analytics
 
through
 
a
 
mobile-first
 
platform
 
built
 
with
 
Flutter
 
for
 
cross-platform
 
compatibility
 
and
 
Supabase
 
for
 
secure,
 
optional
 
cloud
 
storage.
 
By
 
prioritizing
 
offline
 
functionality,
 
intuitive
 
design,
 
and
 
zero
 
hardware
 
requirements
 
beyond
 
a
 
basic
 
smartphone,
 
VendWise
 
aims
 
to
 
democratize
 
access
 
to
 
digital
 
business
 
management
 
tools
 
and
 
enable
 
small
 
vendors
 
to
 
operate
 
with
 
the
 
efficiency
 
and
 
insight
 
previously
 
available
 
only
 
to
 
larger
 
enterprises.

Conceptual
 
Framework
 
 
The
 
conceptual
 
framework
 
of
 
the
 
VendWise
 
Application
 
follows
 
the
 
Input-Process-Output
 
(IPO)

which

flow
 
of

project
 
from
 
conceptualization
 
to
 
implementation.
 
The
 
framework
 
begins
 
with
 
the

of
 
the
 
challenges

by
 
small
 
business
 
owners
 
in
 
managing
 
sales
 
and
 
inventory
 
manually,
 
such
 
as
 
errors
 
in
 
computation,
 
loss
 
of
 
records,
 
and
 
time-consuming
 
bookkeeping.
 
These
 
identified
 
needs
 
serve
 
as
 
the
 
foundation
 
for
 
the
 
design
 
and
 
development
 
of
 
the
 
VendWise
 
system.
 
The
 
Input
 
stage
 
involves
 
gathering
 
requirements
 
through
 
discussions
 
and
 
literature
 
review
 
on
 
point-of-sale
 
systems,
 
identifying
 
user
 
needs,
 
and
 
defining
 
the
 
system
 
objectives.
 
The
 
Process
 
stage
 
focuses
 
on
 
system
 
development
 
using
 
the
 
Waterfall
 
model—covering
 
design,
 
coding,
 
and
 
testing.
 
This
 
includes
 
designing
 
the
 
user
 
interface
 
in
 
Flutter,
 
implementing
 
transaction
 
and
 
inventory
 
logic,
 
and
 
integrating
 
a
 
Supabase
 
database
 
for
 
data
 
management.
 
The
 
Output
 
stage
 
results
 
in
 
a
 
fully
 
functional,
 
lightweight
 
mobile
 
application
 
that
 
allows
 
owners
 
or
 
staff
 
to
 
process
 
sales,
 
automatically
 
update
 
inventory,
 
and
 
generate
 
sales
 
reports
 
without
 
the
 
need
 
for
 
paper
 
receipts.
 
This
 
framework
 
ensures
 
that
 
every
 
stage
 
of
 
system
 
creation
 
is
 
structured,
 
from
 
planning
 
and
 
modeling
 
to
 
the
 
final
 
deployment
 
of
 
a
 
user-friendly
 
and

point-of-sale
 
solution
 
tailored
 
for
 
small
 
businesses.

It

The

,

The
